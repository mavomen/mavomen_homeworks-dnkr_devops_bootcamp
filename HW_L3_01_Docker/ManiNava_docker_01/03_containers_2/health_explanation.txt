# Health Checks Explanation

My test container: health-test, interval 5 s, timeout 3 s, 3 retries.

## 1. What do the statuses mean?

- `starting`: probe not run yet, still in the grace period.
- `healthy`: the last probe succeeded within the interval.
- `unhealthy`: failed more than `--health-retries` times in a row.
- No healthcheck configured => no status shown at all ("none").

## 2. When does the probe run?

First run right after the container starts, then every `--health-interval`
(5 s here) with a per-run `--health-timeout` (3 s). 3 consecutive failures
flip it to `unhealthy`. In health_status.txt you can see it went `healthy`
shortly after start - the flip occured right after the third failed probe.
(I let it keep running and watched it settle.)

## 3. Why are health checks important?

- Docker and orchestrators on top (swarm, k8s, compose) can restart
  unhealthy containers or stop routing traffic to them.
- LB / service discovery can drop unhealthy instances.
- Most importantly it reflects application readiness, not just "process
  alive" - catches hangs, deadlocks and missing dependencies early. In a
  homework demo it's a nice touch; in production it's what keeps the
  rollout sane.
